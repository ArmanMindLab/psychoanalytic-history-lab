# Gap Analysis: ISTDP and Attachment Theory as Moderator of Technique

**Date:** June 20, 2026

## Major Gaps Identified

1. **Lack of technique-specific integration**  
   While some studies show that attachment style moderates outcome in short-term dynamic therapies, there is almost no work that translates this into specific ISTDP technique. How should a therapist adjust the intensity, timing, or form of "pressure", "challenge", and "head-on collision" when the patient has disorganized, avoidant, or preoccupied attachment?

2. **Over-reliance on symptom-focused mediation models**  
   Recent ISTDP trials (e.g. Morris et al., 2025) show that the classical sequential model (break defense → experience affect → symptom reduction) is not supported. Change appears concurrent across domains. This challenges the simple narrative that ISTDP "works because it resolves attachment trauma".

3. **Absence of attachment organization as a treatment planner in ISTDP**  
   ISTDP literature has rich discussion of "fragile", "repressive", and "obsessional" character structures, but very little mapping of these onto contemporary attachment classifications (secure, dismissing, preoccupied, fearful-disorganized). No clear clinical decision tree exists.

4. **Missing comparison with other attachment-informed psychodynamic models**  
   AEDP (Fosha) has explicit attachment-based theory and technique. ISTDP does not — at least not in published form. A rigorous comparison is needed.

## Core Question This Paper Aims to Answer

"How can knowledge of a patient's adult attachment organization be used to tailor the core ISTDP interventions (pressure, challenge, unlocking the unconscious) in real time, and what theoretical refinements to the ISTDP model does this require?"

This remains largely unanswered in the current literature.
