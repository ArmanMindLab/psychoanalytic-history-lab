# Gap Analysis: ISTDP and Attachment Theory

**Date:** June 14, 2026

## Current State of Literature

The intersection of Intensive Short-Term Dynamic Psychotherapy (ISTDP) and Attachment Theory is **emerging but underdeveloped**.

### What exists:
- Strong theoretical overlap: ISTDP explicitly targets attachment trauma, repressed affect, and defensive structures rooted in early caregiver relationships.
- Several recent studies (2024–2025) show ISTDP improves emotion regulation, reduces negative affect, and is moderated by attachment style.
- Defense mechanism research clearly links insecure attachment to immature defenses — the exact target of ISTDP’s “pressure” and “challenge” techniques.

### Major Gaps:

1. **Lack of direct theoretical integration**  
   No major paper has systematically mapped Davanloo’s metapsychology onto modern attachment concepts (e.g., internal working models, earned security, reflective functioning, attachment-based mentalization).

2. **Limited empirical focus on attachment outcomes**  
   Most ISTDP studies measure symptom reduction (depression, anxiety, somatization). Very few measure pre/post changes in adult attachment style or attachment representations.

3. **No comprehensive review paper**  
   There is no current “state of the art” review that brings together:
   - Davanloo’s original theory
   - Contemporary attachment research (Mikulincer, Shaver, Fonagy, Holmes, etc.)
   - ISTDP outcome studies that implicitly or explicitly involve attachment

4. **Gap in clinical technique**  
   How exactly should ISTDP therapists use knowledge of a patient’s attachment style to calibrate pressure, resistance work, and interpretation? This remains largely clinical intuition rather than formalized guidance.
